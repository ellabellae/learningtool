## Problem Statement

Ella reads research papers for fun across many fields (EEG neurofeedback and BCI in Zotero today; economics and ML too) and finds them "so hard to digest." She wants to drop in a PDF and get back something that makes her *understand* the paper, not a summary. Her words: "I don't want a summary. I want it to be like a learning lesson. What is ChatGPT? What are LLMs? What was GPT-3.5? I go through understanding these concepts and then can really understand GPT-4 and the main takeaways. Think of it like a story." Breadth over depth: "not getting really smart about one thing, it is taking hard material to understand and making it digestible."

## What Makes This Cool

- **A paper becomes a story you play through.** Prerequisite rungs first (the concepts the paper assumes that you lack), then the paper as a plot: the world before, what they tried, *your prediction*, what they found, what changed.
- **You guess the ending.** Before the key result, the lesson asks what you think happened. Then the paper's real finding is revealed. That is retrieval practice without a tutor chat.
- **Sliders on the paper's real knobs.** For the 1-3 parameters that actually change the outcome, a widget lets you "change a variable and see what breaks." Every widget is labeled as an illustrative model and sits beside the paper's own sentence about that mechanism.
- **The paper's exact words, one click away.** Every finding points at an extracted sentence with a page number and a highlighted crop of the actual page. The model never writes the quote; it points at it.
- **It remembers what you've read.** Each lesson leaves concept records, marked read when you open the lesson. The next lesson says "you saw this idea in *Connectome-based neurofeedback* (read 2026-03-03), applied there to attention networks."

## Constraints

- Input is PDF with a text layer. Scanned PDFs are out of scope: `extract` fails fast (mean chars per page < 200) with "no text layer; OCR unsupported" and a non-zero exit.
- Personal tool first. Shareable by cloning the repo plus a profile file. No accounts. Requires `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; the README says so.
- LLM calls go through the Anthropic Python SDK with structured output (`messages.parse()` with a Pydantic model; the SDK strips unsupported schema features and validates the response). `claude -p` is rejected because it cannot enforce the schema the checker relies on.
- Files are truth (same pattern as Ella's Atlas tool): profile, spans, lessons, checks, and concept memory are plain JSON/YAML under `data/`. `data/papers/*/source.pdf`, `crops/`, and `lesson.html` (which embeds the crops and runs to several MB) are gitignored, so copyrighted page images never enter the repo; `spans.json`, `lesson.json`, `checks.json` are committed.
- Node 20+ is a runtime requirement, not just a test dependency: `check.py` evaluates widget models through one `node` subprocess per lesson and errors clearly if `node` is missing.
- Papers above 150k characters of span text are refused by `generate` with a message naming the count and "chunking arrives in v1.1". A known v1 limit.
- A lesson is one self-contained HTML file. It cannot write back to the repo. Anything the player records (your prediction) lives in the browser's localStorage and does not feed memory in v1.
- Untrusted input boundaries: Jinja runs with `autoescape=True`; only the inlined widget bundle and CSS are marked safe. The system prompt states that span text is quoted source material, never instructions.
- Git discipline: feature branches, small commits, merges to main, so every change is traceable.
- Tooling on this Mac: Python 3.12, Node 24, bun. PyMuPDF, pymupdf4llm, Pydantic, and the Anthropic SDK are not installed yet; no poppler.
- Generation is one LLM call (2-4 minutes), an audit call, and at most one repair call plus re-audit; worst case about 8 minutes. The stable prompt prefix (spans, registry, memory slugs) is marked cacheable so the audit and repair calls reuse it.

## Premises (agreed)

1. **Breadth over mastery.** Success = "I get what this paper did, why, and what the key knobs are" in 10-15 minutes. Recall and testing are optional deep-mode extras.
2. **The unit of output is a lesson, not a page.** Prerequisite rungs built from the profile, then story scenes: world before + problem, what they tried, your prediction, what they found, what changed + takeaways. Each scene is one screen with an analogy from things you know, an optional widget, and a "paper's words" control. A depth dial (brief / standard / deep) controls how many rungs and scenes you see. *Revised twice in office hours: from "one interactive page" after Ella's pushback, then "predict before reveal" added from the Codex cold read. Codex's "later papers revisit your earlier predictions" is struck from v1 (see localStorage constraint).*
3. **Profile captures learning signals, not learning styles.** Domains you know well (analogy sources), default depth, things to skip, and an explicit "already know this" concept list. Filled by a short survey; a plain editable file. "Learns over time" is deferred until there is usage data. Basis: learning-style matching has near-zero effect (d ≈ 0.04 across meta-analyses; Pashler et al. 2008; Newton 2015); a 2024 ACL study of GPT-4 style-adapted explanations found no clear comprehension gain.
4. **Every finding traces to a source sentence, and a checker pass is v1.** Single-pass LLM explanations get signs, equations, and numbers wrong (Intuitive Papers' experience). Claims are typed: *finding* (must cite spans; structurally checked, then semantically audited), *background* (outside knowledge the paper assumes; carries a citation string that is **not verified in v1** and renders with an "unchecked" tag), *illustrative* (analogy, labeled as such, never checked). The structural checker's pass status is called **cited**, not verified, because it proves pointing and numbers, not meaning; a flag-only audit pass covers meaning.
5. **Widgets come from a reviewed template library.** The LLM picks a template id and fills parameters, slider ranges, and labels; trusted code implements the math and owns the hard bounds. Generated code is never executed. Every widget must cite the paper's sentence describing the mechanism it models, and the audit judges whether that text supports the template's shape (a two-group comparison never gets a smooth curve). Papers whose mechanism has no supported template get prose only in v1 (the paper's own figures are v1.1). *Revised from "LLM builds self-contained widgets" after the office-hours cold read, and hardened in eng review.*
6. **Personal first, no accounts.** Product questions are later questions.
7. **Every paper leaves concept records.** Lessons tie back to prior papers by exact concept-id match, and only to papers you have actually opened. A cross-paper concept map is the v2 feature this enables.

## Approaches Considered

- **B: Local web app (FastAPI + Vite player, PDF.js highlights, streaming scenes, library view).** Completeness 10/10. Ella chose B, then reversed to A. Deferred: it is the upgrade path once the lesson format is stable across ~5 papers or a non-technical friend needs it. Decision logged (id 5fa11115).
- **C: marimo notebook as the player.** Rejected: notebook layout fights scene navigation and the quote panel.

## Recommended Approach

**A: Python pipeline, HTML lesson files.**

### Pipeline

```
 learn extract <pdf>          learn generate <id>            learn audit <id>        learn check <id>
 ┌──────────────────┐         ┌──────────────────────┐       ┌────────────────┐      ┌─────────────────────┐
 │ pymupdf4llm      │ spans   │ ONE structured call  │lesson │ flag-only LLM  │flags │ rules 1-7, pure     │ checks
 │ reading order +  │───────▶ │ spans+registry+slugs │──────▶│ pass: claims,  │─────▶│ Python + one node   │───────┐
 │ word boxes →     │  .json  │ (cacheable prefix)   │ .json │ prose, widget  │      │ call; pass = zero   │ .json │
 │ sentence spans   │         │ + profile + depth    │       │ template fit   │      │ failures, ≥1 finding│       │
 └──────────────────┘         └──────────────────────┘       └────────────────┘      └─────────────────────┘       │
                                        ▲                                                       │ fail (once)     │
                                        │ learn repair <id>: previous lesson + failure list     │                 │
                                        └──────── replacements keyed by claim/widget/scene id ◀─┘                 │
                                                                                                                  ▼
 learn open <id>  ◀──────  learn render <id>: lesson + checks (hash must match) + profile → lesson.html  ◀────────┘
 sets read_date            autoescape, TEMPLATES[id] wrapper, base64 crops, badges, flags
```

`learn lesson <pdf>` chains extract → generate → audit → check → (repair → audit → check, once) → render → open. Render and open always run; the exit code is the last check's; memory records are written only by a passing check, and `read_date` only by `open`.

### Layout

```
learningtool/
  learn                    # CLI entry (see Commands)
  learningtool/
    schema.py              # Pydantic models below + validators
    profile.py             # load/validate profile.yaml with field-level error messages
    extract.py             # pymupdf4llm page_chunks (words) -> sentence spans with per-line bboxes; crops
    generate.py            # one structured-output call -> LessonDraft; post-fill; truncation guard
    audit.py               # flag-only semantic audit call -> audit flags (feed check/repair)
    check.py               # rules 1-7 -> checks.json (stamped with lesson_sha256); memory upsert on pass
    repair.py              # previous lesson + failures -> replacements by id; merge
    render.py              # lesson + checks + profile -> lesson.html (Jinja autoescape, inline CSS/JS, base64 crops)
    memory.py              # concepts.json read/upsert; read_date on open; tie-back lookup
    prompts/               # lesson.md, audit.md, repair.md (plain text, versioned)
  widgets/
    registry.py            # loads every templates/<id>/manifest.json
    templates/<id>/manifest.json   # params, hard bounds, named outputs, labels — the single contract
    templates/<id>/model.mjs       # exports model(params) -> {output: number}, curve(params, xs), draw()
  player/                  # scene player JS/CSS inlined into every lesson.html
  data/
    profile.yaml           # created by `learn survey`; profile.example.yaml is committed
    papers/<sha256>/
      source.pdf (ignored), spans.json, crops/ (ignored)
      lessons/<profile_name>/{lesson.json, audit.json, checks.json, lesson.html (ignored)}
    memory/concepts.json
  tests/                   # unit, E2E (headless browser on lesson.html), eval (opt-in), fixtures/build_fixture.py
  docs/designs/            # this document
  docs/spikes/             # 001-does-it-teach.md
```

### Commands

`<id>` is the sha256 of the PDF; any unique prefix of 8+ characters is accepted. `--profile <path>` (default `data/profile.yaml`) selects the profile; its `name` field selects the lessons subdirectory.

- `learn survey [--profile]` writes the profile. Any command that needs one errors with "run `learn survey`" if missing; a malformed profile prints the field name and value, never a traceback.
- `learn extract <pdf>` copies the PDF to `data/papers/<sha256>/source.pdf` and writes `spans.json`. Skips if `spans.json` exists unless `--force`. Fails on no text layer.
- `learn generate <id>` writes `lessons/<profile>/lesson.json`. Refuses papers over 150k span characters. Detects `stop_reason == max_tokens` and exits with "lesson too long for one call (N tokens); chunking arrives in v1.1". SDK client: `max_retries=3`, timeout 600 s; on final failure prints the error class and "re-run learn generate <id>".
- `learn audit <id>` runs the flag-only semantic pass and writes `audit.json`: for every finding, "does the claim follow from its cited sentences"; for every scene's prose, "does anything contradict the cited sentences"; for every widget, "does the mechanism text support a relationship of this template's shape". The audit can only raise flags, never clear a structural failure.
- `learn check <id>` writes `checks.json` (with `lesson_sha256`), never modifying `lesson.json`. Exit 0 only when there are zero failures of any kind (claims, widgets, references, audit flags) and at least one `finding`. On exit 0 it replaces this paper's records in `concepts.json` (delete all for `paper_id`, then insert, preserving any existing `created_at` and `read_date`).
- `learn repair <id>` sends the previous `lesson.json` plus the failure list (claim ids, widget ids, scene ids, reasons) and receives replacements keyed by id; whole scenes may be replaced for scene-level failures. Untouched items stay byte-identical.
- `learn render <id>` reads `lesson.json`, `checks.json`, and the profile; refuses a `checks.json` whose `lesson_sha256` does not match ("re-run learn check"); exits 1 if `source.pdf` is missing ("re-run learn extract <pdf>"); applies removals and flags; writes `lesson.html` with `profile.default_depth` baked in.
- `learn open <id>` opens `lesson.html` and sets `read_date` on this paper's concept records if unset.
- `learn list` shows papers, profiles, and unread lessons.
- `learn lesson <pdf>` chains the above as drawn in the pipeline.

### Data shapes (versioned, Pydantic-validated)

`Scene` is flat, no unions; dicts are lists of named records so the structured-output schema stays inside the API's supported subset (no recursion, no complex enums, `additionalProperties: false`). `feat/scaffold` validates the emitted JSON schema with the SDK before any prompt is written.

```
Profile       {schema_version, name, known_domains[str], default_depth: brief|standard|deep, skip_topics[str], known_concepts[str]}
Span          {id, page, text, bboxes[[x0,y0,x1,y1]]}   # sentence-level, one page per span (a sentence crossing a page or column break is split); text dehyphenated and ligature-folded; one bbox per rendered line
LessonDraft   {title, scenes[Scene], widgets[Widget], concepts[ConceptRecord]}        # what the generate call emits
Lesson        LessonDraft + {schema_version, paper_id, profile_name, prior_links[PriorLink]}   # generate.py post-fills
Scene         {id, role: prereq|before|problem|tried|predict|found|changed, min_depth: brief|standard|deep,
               title, analogy, prose, claims[Claim], widget_id?,
               prediction_question?, prediction_options[str], prediction_answer_index?, reveal_scene_id?}
Claim         {id, text, kind: finding|background|illustrative, quote_span_id?, span_ids[str], citation?}
Widget        {id, template_id, params[{name, value}], ranges[{name, lo, hi}], labels[{name, text}],
               evidence_span_ids[str], mechanism_span_ids[str],
               expected_behaviors[{param, param_delta: +|-, output, output_delta: +|-}]}
ConceptRecord {concept_id (slug), name, one_line, scene_id}
PriorLink     {scene_id, concept_id, paper_id, paper_title, read_date, note}
AuditFlags    {schema_version, lesson_sha256, claims[{claim_id, contradicts: bool, reason}],
               scenes[{scene_id, contradicts: bool, reason}], widgets[{widget_id, template_fits: bool, reason}]}
Checks        {schema_version, paper_id, profile_name, lesson_sha256,
               claims[{claim_id, status: cited|failed|unchecked, reason?}],
               widgets[{widget_id, ok, reason?}], scenes[{scene_id, flags[str], widget_removed, reveal_nulled}]}
RepairRequest {lesson: Lesson, failures[{kind: claim|widget|scene, id, reason}]}
RepairReply   {claims[Claim], widgets[Widget], scenes[Scene]}     # replacements keyed by id
```

Statuses live only in `checks.json`; `lesson.json` on disk is the model's output plus post-filled fields, never mutated by `check`. `scenes[]` order is play order. Validators: every `prereq` scene precedes every story scene; unique `Claim.id`, `Scene.id`, `Widget.id`; every `ConceptRecord.scene_id` resolves; a `predict` scene has a question, ≥ 2 options, and a `reveal_scene_id` resolving to a `found` scene with the same `min_depth` (`prediction_answer_index` optional; null means free-form guess); scenes with `min_depth: brief` include at least one each of `problem`, `tried`, `found`, `changed`. The player finds the predict scene whose `reveal_scene_id` equals the current scene id to show right/wrong.

Quotes are whole spans in v1: a finding's "paper's words" is `spans[quote_span_id].text`. Offsets were dropped because a model cannot count characters reliably; sub-sentence quotes are v1.1. `profile.known_concepts` are free-text names listed in the prompt with "do not emit a prerequisite for these".

`concepts.json` is `[{concept_id, name, one_line, paper_id, paper_title, scene_id, created_at, read_date?}]`. A passing check deletes and re-inserts the paper's records, carrying over `created_at` and `read_date`. `generate.py` fills `Lesson.prior_links` by matching draft concept slugs against records from other papers that have a `read_date`.

### Depth dial

Generate `deep` once. Every scene carries `min_depth`. The player filters client-side: `brief` shows brief scenes, `standard` adds standard, `deep` shows all. The dial is a view, not a regeneration; the brief-arc validator guarantees a complete story at every setting.

### Quotes and crops

The "paper's words" for a finding is `spans[quote_span_id].text`; when null, `span_ids[0]`'s text and crop. `render` inlines one base64 PNG crop per cited span (deduplicated) at 110 DPI: the union of the span's `bboxes` padded by 24 pt and clipped to the page, with the highlight drawn from the `bboxes`. Scenes with no `finding` claims render "No paper's words for this rung".

### Widgets

The LLM emits `template_id`, params, ranges, labels, `evidence_span_ids` (the paper's result this toy is next to), `mechanism_span_ids` (the paper's description of the mechanism the template models), and expected behaviors. The player shows an "illustrative model, not this study's measurements" badge and the evidence sentence under every chart. Each template is a directory with `manifest.json` (the single contract: param names, hard bounds, named outputs, labels) and `model.mjs`; `registry.py` loads the manifests, `render` passes each manifest to its `.mjs` at runtime as `TEMPLATES["<id>"] = (() => { ...; return {model, curve, draw}; })(manifest)`. First templates: `threshold-learning-curve`, `before-after-groups`.

### Checker rules (v1)

1. Every `finding` has ≥ 1 existing `span_ids`; `quote_span_id`, if set, is one of them. Every widget's `evidence_span_ids` and `mechanism_span_ids` exist and are non-empty. Otherwise `failed`.
2. Numeric tokens in a `finding`'s `text` appear in its cited spans' `text`. Regex `-?\d[\d,]*(\.\d+)?|-?\.\d+`; thousands separators stripped; trailing zeros stripped only after a decimal point; `.05` equals `0.05`; no unit or word normalization. Otherwise `failed`. "twelve" vs "12" false failures are accepted and documented.
3. `background` claims with a non-empty `citation` → `unchecked`; empty → `failed`.
4. `illustrative` claims → `unchecked`.
5. `template_id` exists in the registry; every param and range name is declared in its manifest; every ranged param has a value with `lo <= value <= hi`; every range is inside the manifest's hard bounds; every referenced output is a manifest output. Failure: `checks.widgets[].ok = false`, scene flagged `widget_removed`, render omits the widget.
6. Every `expected_behavior` holds strictly (`+` means output at `hi` > output at `lo`; equality fails), evaluated by one batched `node` call over all widgets. Failure as rule 5.
7. `reveal_scene_id`, `widget_id`, `ConceptRecord.scene_id` resolve within the lesson; every `prior_links` `(paper_id, concept_id)` resolves to a `concepts.json` record with a `read_date`. Dangling references are recorded (`reveal_nulled`, or the link dropped) and the scene flagged.
8. Audit flags (from `audit.json` for the same `lesson_sha256`): a claim with `contradicts` → `failed`; a scene with `contradicts` → flagged `prose_contradicts`; a widget with `template_fits = false` → as rule 5.

`cited` = structurally checked (pointing and numbers), not meaning. `failed` = rejected. `unchecked` = not checkable by design. A flagged scene renders with a visible marker on the scene and its arc dot; the lesson never renders silently clean.

### Repair

`learn repair` sends `RepairRequest` and merges `RepairReply` by id: replaced claims, widgets, and scenes overwrite their originals; everything else is byte-identical. The repair prompt shares the cacheable prefix with generate and audit. Audit and check run again on the merged lesson; a second failure renders with flags and exits 1.

### Memory

Records are upserted by a passing check and marked `read_date` by `learn open`. Tie-backs only reference papers with a `read_date`, so the tool never claims you read something you only generated. `learn list` shows unread lessons.

### Player

From the approved wireframe `~/.gstack/projects/ellabellae-learningtool/designs/mockup-20260912/wireframe-scene.png`: story arc as top navigation (italic dots for prerequisite rungs, a marker on flagged scenes), scene on the left (analogy prose, then widget with badge and evidence row), "The paper's words" on the right (quote, page, crop with highlight, claim list with kind and status tags), depth dial and Next always visible, tie-back line under the scene when `prior_links` is non-empty. The prediction scene records the choice in localStorage (wrapped in try/catch; private mode degrades to no persistence); the reveal scene shows right/wrong when `prediction_answer_index` is set.

### Extraction

pymupdf4llm `to_markdown(page_chunks=True, extract_words=True)` provides reading order across columns and per-word boxes. `extract.py` then: drops header/footer words (top and bottom 6% of page height), joins words into lines and lines into text with end-of-line dehyphenation while recording an offset table per line, splits sentences by regex, maps each sentence's range back to its lines for `bboxes`, splits any sentence that crosses a page, and stops at the first heading matching References/Bibliography. Ligatures are folded by PyMuPDF's text flags. A visual spot-check script renders every span's highlight on its page so extraction is judged by eye on the first real paper.

- **Chunking for papers over 150k characters**: v1.1; the truncation message is the trigger.
- **Approach B web app** (streaming, PDF.js, accounts-free sharing to non-Python friends): decision 5fa11115.
- **OCR, Socratic deep mode, automatic preference learning, concept-map UI**: v2.

- **Nightly eval in CI**: rejected in 8A; the eval is opt-in and local.
- **Fitting template parameters to the paper's data points**: rejected in 3C; the badge and evidence row are the honest v1.

| Codepath | Realistic failure | Test | Handling | User sees |

| extract | scanned PDF, no text layer | yes | guard, exit 1 | "no text layer; OCR unsupported" |
